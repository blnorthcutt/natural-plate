## Description

An unfussy little cake with a top layer of jammy, macerated strawberries, so gooey it's best served — you guessed it — with a spoon. The batter comes together in one bowl with basic pantry ingredients and just a small handful of berries, fresh or frozen, and the whole thing bakes in about 25 minutes. Macerating and mashing the fruit releases every drop of juice, which sinks into the cake and keeps it extra moist as it bakes. Adapted from the NYT Cooking original by Jerrelle Guy — serve it warm with vanilla ice cream and burrow in.

## Instructions

1. **Heat the oven.** Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease an 8-inch square or round baking dish with butter. Set aside.
2. **Macerate the berries.** Using your hands or the back of a fork, mash the strawberries to release all their juices, then stir in ⅓ cup of the brown sugar. Set aside. (If using frozen berries, defrost them first — a quick spin in the microwave works — and drain off the excess liquid before mashing.)
3. **Make the batter.** In a medium bowl, whisk together the melted butter, the remaining ⅓ cup brown sugar, the milk and salt, then add the flour and baking powder and continue whisking just until the batter is smooth. Transfer the batter (it's not much) to the greased dish and spread it evenly into the corners.
4. **Assemble and bake.** Spoon the strawberries and all their juices over the top of the batter. Bake for 20 to 25 minutes, just until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Remove from the oven and let cool for 3 to 5 minutes before spooning into bowls. Serve warm with scoops of vanilla ice cream.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best enjoyed warm from the oven. Leftovers keep in the fridge for a day or two, covered — reheat briefly in the microwave or oven before serving.
- **Variations:** The cake is a riff on an old-fashioned "Essie's Cobbler" — any juicy fruit works in place of strawberries, including peaches or blueberries (NYT readers say bumping up the fruit amount only makes it better). Swap light brown sugar for muscovado sugar, brighten the berries with a little lemon zest, or add vanilla and a pinch of cinnamon to the batter. Sprinkle freshly ground cardamom or ground ginger over the top before baking, or scatter ribbons of fresh basil over the hot cake once it comes out of the oven.
- **Helpful fix:** One of the most-loved reader tweaks: melt the butter in the baking dish while the oven preheats, leaving a little in the pan, then pour in the batter — the hot pan creates a lovely chewy crust along the bottom and sides.
- **Serving suggestion:** Scoop it warm into bowls with vanilla ice cream; it also disappears fast as an unexpected brunch treat.
