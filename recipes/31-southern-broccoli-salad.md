## Description

This classic Southern broccoli salad is a staple at potlucks, picnics, and family gatherings across the Carolinas and beyond. Crisp blanched broccoli is tossed in a creamy, tangy dressing with sharp Cheddar, sweet golden raisins, and crunchy sunflower seeds, then topped with crispy bacon. It comes together in just 15 minutes and only gets better as it chills — the perfect make-ahead side for summer cookouts, or a quick lunch on its own.

## Instructions

1. **Blanch the broccoli.** Bring a large saucepan of salted water to a boil. Add the broccoli and cook until bright green, about 1 minute. Rinse under cold water until cool (or transfer to an ice bath). Drain well and pat dry.

2. **Cook the bacon.** Heat a large nonstick skillet over medium. Add the bacon and cook, turning occasionally, until golden and crispy, 7 to 8 minutes. Transfer to a paper towel-lined plate to drain. Once cool, chop into bite-size pieces. Discard the bacon fat or reserve for another use.

3. **Make the dressing.** In a large bowl, combine the mayonnaise, red onion, apple cider vinegar, and sugar. Whisk until smooth.

4. **Assemble the salad.** Add the broccoli, cheddar, golden raisins, and sunflower seeds to the bowl with the dressing. Season with salt and pepper and toss until well combined. (If time allows, refrigerate for 30 minutes to 3 hours to let the flavors meld, stirring occasionally.)

5. **Serve.** Transfer the salad to a serving bowl or platter. Top with the chopped bacon. Serve immediately, or refrigerate and serve chilled.

## Notes

- **Mayo adjustment:** Several readers note that 1 cup of mayonnaise is more than needed — start with 2 heaping tablespoons and add more to taste. The dressing is easily adjusted.
- **Make-ahead:** The salad can be refrigerated for 30 minutes to 3 hours before serving to let the flavors meld. Stir occasionally. It keeps for a few days in the fridge.
- **Variations:** Substitute dried cranberries for the golden raisins and pepitas (pumpkin seeds) for the sunflower seeds. For a different texture, cut the cheddar into tiny cubes instead of grating. Some readers skip the cheese and add a teaspoon of Dijon mustard to the dressing. Instead of blanching, try a quick stir-fry of the broccoli in the bacon pan. For a Pennsylvania Dutch twist, use a vinegar-based dressing and omit the cheese.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve chilled alongside grilled meats, pulled pork, or fried chicken for a classic cookout spread. It also makes a satisfying lunch on its own.
