## Description

This late-night Roman staple is astonishingly full-flavored for how few ingredients it uses. Pasta Aglio, Olio e Peperoncino — garlic, oil, and chile pasta — is a minimalist masterpiece from Mark Bittman. The sauce comes together in under 10 minutes while the pasta cooks: garlic and fresh peperoncini sizzle in olive oil, then a splash of starchy pasta water is stirred in to create a light, emulsified coating. The key is technique — undercook the pasta just shy of al dente, then finish it in the pan with vigorous stirring and reserved pasta water to build a silky, clingy sauce. It's weeknight magic that proves simple doesn't mean plain.

## Instructions

1. **Start the pasta water.** Put a large pot of generously salted water on to boil.
2. **Prepare the chiles.** Remove the stems from the peperoncini, halve them lengthwise, then slice them crosswise into ¼-inch pieces.
3. **Cook the aromatics.** Put the olive oil, sliced garlic, and prepared peperoncini in a large deep pan set over medium heat (not medium-high — the garlic can scorch). Cook until the garlic is translucent and golden, 2 to 3 minutes. Add the chopped parsley to the pan, then turn off the heat and let the oil cool for a couple of minutes.
4. **Cook the pasta.** Meanwhile, cook the pasta until it is nearly al dente — still a bit chalky and firm in the center. Drain, reserving 1 cup of the cooking liquid.
5. **Build the sauce.** Once the oil has cooled slightly, add ½ cup of the reserved pasta water to the pan. Return to high heat and reduce the liquid by about half, stirring occasionally.
6. **Finish the pasta.** Add the undercooked pasta to the pan and stir vigorously as it continues to cook. Add the remaining pasta water a tablespoon or two at a time as needed to keep the sauce loose and help the pasta finish cooking. The constant stirring emulsifies the oil and starch into a creamy, clinging sauce. Season with salt to taste and serve immediately.

## Notes

- **Watch the heat:** The most common pitfall is burnt garlic. Keep the heat at medium, not medium-high, and pull the pan off the heat the moment the garlic turns pale gold — it will continue cooking in the hot oil.
- **On the oil:** Several readers find ⅔ cup yields a very oily dish. Start with ½ cup and add more if needed; you can always drizzle in extra at the table.
- **Peperoncini substitutes:** Fresh or dried small red chiles are ideal, but crushed red pepper flakes (the kind found at pizzerias) work beautifully. Use about 1 to 2 teaspoons to taste.
- **Anchovy variation:** A classic Roman twist — mash 2 or 3 high-quality anchovy fillets into the cooled oil before adding the pasta water. The anchovies melt into the sauce, adding deep umami without tasting fishy.
- **Storage:** Best eaten immediately — this dish does not reheat well. If you must, toss leftovers with a splash of water and reheat gently in a pan.
- **Serving suggestion:** Top with toasted fresh breadcrumbs tossed with salt, pepper, and grated Parmesan or Pecorino for crunch. A squeeze of lemon brightens it up nicely.
