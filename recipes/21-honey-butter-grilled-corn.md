## Description

Kenji López-Alt's clever grilling technique: instead of brushing corn with butter at the end, the cobs get a char on the hot side of the grill, then a soak in a side pan of simmering honey butter that reduces into a glossy, sweet-spicy glaze. The pan keeps the corn hot and juicy for up to 45 minutes, which makes it perfect for cookouts, potlucks, and summer dinners. Mild Korean chile flakes add a gentle smoky heat — omit them or swap in ancho chile powder to suit your crowd.

## Instructions

1. **Make the butter bath.** Combine the honey, butter, chile flakes, 1 cup water and 1 teaspoon salt in a deep 9-by-13-inch disposable aluminum tray (or a similarly sized stovetop-safe baking dish you don't mind getting dirty).
2. **Set up the grill.** Adjust the burners on a gas grill, or arrange the hot coals on a charcoal grill, to build a fire that is hot on one side and medium-low on the other. Place the tray on the cooler side, cover the grill, and let it heat for a few minutes, swirling occasionally, until the butter is melted and the liquid simmers gently.
3. **Char the corn.** Add the corn to the hot side of the grill and cook, turning occasionally, until the kernels are lightly blackened in spots all over, 5 to 10 minutes.
4. **Glaze the corn.** Transfer the corn to the tray and turn to coat in the liquid. Keep turning as the liquid reduces to a shiny glaze, 10 to 12 minutes. Once glazed, the corn can be held in the pan for up to another 30 to 45 minutes — just turn it occasionally and top up with ¼ cup water as needed to keep the honey and butter from burning. Meanwhile, the hot side of the grill is free for whatever else you're cooking.
5. **Finish and serve.** Right before serving, give the corn a final coat in the glaze, sprinkle with the minced parsley or chives, and transfer to a serving platter.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Leftover corn keeps in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Warm it back up in a covered skillet with a splash of water, or eat cold — the glaze is delicious either way.
- **Make-ahead:** The real beauty of this recipe — the glazed corn holds in the warm pan for 30 to 45 minutes, so you can grill it ahead of everything else and serve all at once.
- **Variations:** Go sweet-hot Korean style with 2 tablespoons gochujang, a few minced garlic cloves, and a toasted sesame seed finish. For sake-miso, add ½ cup sake, 2 tablespoons light miso, and 2 tablespoons soy sauce to the bath. Or blend a couple of canned chipotle chiles with lime juice into the glaze and finish with cilantro or mint. Any chile flakes work, or skip them entirely — and if you'd rather not use a disposable tray, a baking dish or cast-iron pan with a tall lip works just as well (commenters confirm).
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve alongside burgers, ribs, or grilled chicken; with lime wedges for squeezing. Slurp-worthy leftover glaze can be spooned over the corn or used as a dip for bread.
