## Description

This NYT Cooking recipe from Ali Slagle turns tomato salad into a meal by marrying creamy beans with the briny, salty hallmarks of pasta puttanesca: tomatoes, capers, olives and garlic. Coarsely chopped pebbles of Parmesan add bursts of umami, and while it's not traditional to puttanesca, the salad would be just as at home at a summer cookout as it is in a lunchbox. There's no cooking — just stir everything together and let it sit for 10 minutes or up to 2 hours, during which the tomato juices mingle with the oil, olives and capers and the beans drink it all up. Omit the cheese for a vegan version.

## Instructions

1. **Combine everything.** In a large bowl, stir together the tomatoes, chickpeas (or white beans, or a mix), parsley, Parmesan, olive oil, olives, capers, lemon juice and garlic. Season lightly with kosher salt and stir once more.
2. **Let it rest.** Let the salad sit at room temperature for at least 10 minutes, or up to 2 hours, so the flavors meld and the beans soak up the dressing. The salad is good right away and only gets better with time.
3. **Taste and adjust.** Before serving, taste and add more salt and lemon juice until it tastes bright — the balance depends largely on your tomatoes. Serve at room temperature.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Keeps in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 2 days, and the flavors keep deepening as it sits. Bring to room temperature before serving.
- **Make-ahead:** A perfect make-ahead salad — the 10-minute rest is the minimum, but 30 minutes to 2 hours (or overnight in the fridge) is even better. Great for work lunches, picnics and potlucks.
- **Variations:** The recipe is endlessly adaptable. Swap the Parmesan for feta or crumbled goat cheese, or omit it entirely (a vegan Parmesan like Violife's wedge works well) for a vegan version. White beans — cannellini, great northern or giant limas — work beautifully in place of chickpeas. Many commenters add sliced red onion, a handful of arugula, toasted walnuts, or red pepper flakes for heat; others bulk it out with tinned tuna or a spoonful of anchovy paste for extra puttanesca flavor. Basil makes a lovely stand-in for the parsley. For mellower garlic, grate the clove into the lemon juice first and let it sit while you prep the rest.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve as a main-course salad with crusty bread for soaking up the juices, or as a side for grilled chicken, fish or burgers. Doubles as a chunky bruschetta topping.
