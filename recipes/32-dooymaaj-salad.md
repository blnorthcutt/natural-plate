## Description

A refreshing, zesty, herb-filled bread salad inspired by dooymaaj, a nostalgic Iranian childhood snack of dried, days-old bread combined with fresh herbs, cheese and walnuts — an effort not to waste a single crumb. This version turns the beloved snack into a bright, modern salad: crisp toasted lavash croutons tossed with handfuls of parsley, mint and basil, crumbled feta and walnuts, all bound by a creamy-tangy buttermilk dressing. Hearty enough to stand alone as a light meal, or serve alongside grilled chicken or fish. Adapted from Naz Deravian's recipe for NYT Cooking; note that the finished dish is a dressed salad, not the rolled balls of milk and butter described in the recipe's nostalgic introduction.

## Instructions

1. **Toast the lavash.** Position a rack in the center of the oven and heat the oven to 400°F/200°C. Place the lavash directly on the rack and toast until crisp, about 3 minutes. Don't let it take on color or burn. Let cool completely, then break into 2-inch pieces — the pieces are better if they're not uniform. Use immediately, or store in an airtight container for up to 3 days.
2. **Make the dressing.** In a medium bowl, whisk together the red wine vinegar, dried mint, salt and pepper until the salt is dissolved. Drizzle in the olive oil, add the buttermilk and whisk well to combine. Use immediately, or refrigerate for up to 4 hours.
3. **Finish the salad.** Place the parsley, mint, basil, scallions, feta and walnuts in a large bowl. Scatter the toasts on top. Add three-quarters of the dressing and gently toss to combine; the salad should be generously and evenly dressed. If needed, add the remaining dressing, then taste and adjust the seasonings. Serve right away so the lavash stays crisp.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best eaten immediately — the lavash softens as the salad sits. The toasted lavash pieces keep in an airtight container for up to 3 days and the dressing refrigerates for up to 4 hours, but toss everything together only at serving time.
- **Make-ahead:** Toast the bread and whisk the dressing ahead of time, then combine at the last minute so the croutons stay crisp.
- **Variations:** Don't overthink the herbs — readers say grab whatever parsley, mint and basil you have (dill, cilantro and tarragon also work), or use a bag of pita chips in place of lavash. No buttermilk? Thin Greek yogurt with a splash of milk and a dash of vinegar. Several commenters add butter lettuce, diced cucumber or sliced radishes to stretch it, and a sprinkle of sumac at the end brightens it further.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve on its own as a light meal, or alongside grilled chicken or fish, such as joojeh kababs or saffron salmon kababs.
