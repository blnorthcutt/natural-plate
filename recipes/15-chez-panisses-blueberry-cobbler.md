## Description

A classic from the kitchen of Chez Panisse that lets the berries shine: just ⅓ cup of sugar for the filling, and free-form biscuit patties arranged so the bubbling blueberry juice can surge up around them. The topping is a barely-sweetened cream biscuit — crisp on the outside, tender inside — and the whole thing bakes in one dish in under an hour. Serve it warm with a pitcher of cream or a scoop of vanilla ice cream for the perfect end-of-summer dessert.

## Instructions

1. **Heat the oven.** Heat the oven to 375°F.
2. **Season the berries.** In a bowl, toss the blueberries with the ⅓ cup sugar and 1 tablespoon flour. Set aside while you make the dough.
3. **Make the dough.** In a separate bowl, mix the 1½ cups flour, salt, 1½ tablespoons sugar and baking powder. Cut in the cold butter with a pastry blender or your fingertips until the mixture resembles coarse meal. Add the cream and mix lightly, just until the dry ingredients are moistened — don't overwork it, or the biscuits will be tough.
4. **Assemble the cobbler.** Put the berries in a 1½-quart gratin or baking dish. Divide the dough into patties 2 to 2½ inches in diameter and ½ inch thick, and arrange them over the top of the berries, leaving gaps so the juice can bubble through.
5. **Bake.** Bake until the topping is golden brown and the berry juices bubble thickly around the dough, 35 to 40 minutes.
6. **Serve.** Let cool slightly, then serve warm, with extra cream to pour over the top, if desired.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best the day it's made; leftovers keep, covered, in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently in a 350°F oven before serving.
- **Make-ahead:** The berry mixture can be tossed up to a few hours ahead, and the dough can be mixed and chilled for up to 1 hour before baking — but bake the cobbler fresh for the best biscuits.
- **Variations:** NYT readers swear by brightening the berries with the zest and juice of half a lemon (plus a pinch of cinnamon) before baking — it makes the blueberry flavor pop. Swap the heavy cream for buttermilk in the dough for a tangier biscuit. For extra crunch, brush the patties with cream and sprinkle with demerara sugar before baking.
- **Helpful fix:** If your berries are very juicy, give them a quick simmer on the stove before baking — otherwise they can take a long time to thicken and the biscuits may over-brown. A small but deep 8x8 dish and thick patties give the best biscuit-to-berry ratio.
- **Serving suggestion:** Pour cream over the warm cobbler straight from the pitcher, or serve with whipped cream or vanilla ice cream.
