## Description

A classic Tuscan bread-thickened vegetable stew that's hearty enough to be a meal on its own. Ribollita means "reboiled" — it was originally a way to use up leftover bread and vegetables, and it only gets better the next day. This version from Mark Bittman packs a full pound of kale into a tomatoey bean-and-vegetable base, topped with toasted bread, red onion, and Parmesan that bakes into a golden, crisp crust. Serve it steaming from the oven for a cozy autumn or winter dinner.

## Instructions

1. **Sauté the aromatics.** Put 2 tablespoons of the olive oil in a large ovenproof pot over medium heat. When it's hot, add the onion, carrot, celery, and garlic; sprinkle with salt and pepper and cook, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are soft, 5 to 10 minutes.
2. **Heat the oven.** Heat the oven to 500°F (260°C) with a rack in the upper third.
3. **Build the stew.** Drain the beans (rinse if canned) and add them to the pot along with the tomatoes and their juices, the vegetable stock, rosemary, and thyme. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat so the soup bubbles steadily. Cover and cook, stirring once or twice to break up the tomatoes, until the flavors meld, 15 to 20 minutes.
4. **Add the greens.** Fish out and discard the rosemary and thyme stems, if you like. Stir in the kale and cook until just wilted, about 2 minutes. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
5. **Top and bake.** Lay the toasted bread slices on top of the stew so they cover the surface and overlap as little as possible. Scatter the sliced red onion over the top, drizzle with the remaining 3 tablespoons olive oil, and sprinkle with the Parmesan. Transfer the pot to the oven and bake until the bread, onions, and cheese are browned and crisp, 10 to 15 minutes. (If your pot fits under the broiler, you can brown the top there instead.)
6. **Serve.** Divide the soup and bread among 4 bowls and serve hot.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Ribollita gets even better as it sits. Keep in the fridge for up to 5 days. Freezes well for up to 3 months.
- **Make-ahead:** The traditional Tuscan way is to make this a day ahead — the bread breaks down and thickens the soup beautifully. Cook through step 4, let it cool, and refrigerate overnight. The next day, reheat gently, then proceed with the topping and baking. The name "ribollita" literally means "reboiled."
- **Variations:** Use escarole in place of kale. For a vegan version, omit the Parmesan or substitute with nutritional yeast. Add a splash of red wine along with the stock for deeper flavor. Some readers recommend adding diced sweet potato or butternut squash for extra heft, or doubling the carrots and celery. A spoonful of tomato paste stirred in after the aromatics soften adds richness.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve with a drizzle of good extra-virgin olive oil and a side of crusty bread. A glass of Chianti pairs perfectly.
