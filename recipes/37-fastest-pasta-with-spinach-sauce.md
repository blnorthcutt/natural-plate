## Description

Sometimes the very best pasta is the simplest, and this one takes that idea to its logical end: the "sauce" is just garlic, olives, red pepper flakes and olive oil stirred together at the bottom of a bowl, while the spinach wilts in the same pot as the pasta — no extra pans, no simmering. Developed by Jack Bishop of "The Complete Vegetarian Cookbook" and streamlined by Mark Bittman, it's a fast, briny, garlicky weeknight dinner that comes together in about 25 minutes with just one pot and a colander. Serve it right away while the greens are still bright.

## Instructions

1. **Start the pasta water.** Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it generously (use at least a gallon of water per pound of pasta — the pot must be big enough to hold the greens, and too little water will slow things down when the spinach goes in).
2. **Make the olive mixture.** Meanwhile, mince the garlic as finely as possible and combine it in the bottom of a warm bowl with the red pepper flakes, the roughly chopped olives, and the olive oil.
3. **Cook the pasta and wilt the spinach.** Add the pasta to the boiling water and cook until it is nearly done, testing by taste. Plunge the spinach into the water and cook until it wilts, less than a minute.
4. **Drain and toss.** Drain quickly, allowing some cooking water to cling to the pasta and spinach, then toss everything in the bowl with the garlic-olive mixture.
5. **Season and serve.** Season with salt and freshly ground black pepper, and serve immediately.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best eaten right away while the pasta is warm and the spinach is bright. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 2 days; refresh with a splash of olive oil or lemon juice when reheating.
- **Make-ahead:** The garlic-olive mixture can be combined in the bowl up to an hour ahead so dinner is ready the second the pasta is done.
- **Variations:** Chop the spinach chiffonade-style (roll the leaves into a cigar and cut across) for pretty ribbons that don't clump. Swap in Castelvetrano olives for a milder, buttery flavor and triple the garlic for extra punch. A squeeze of lemon juice stirred into the olive mixture plus a shower of freshly grated Parmesan brightens everything. Some readers add golden raisins (sultanas) and toasted walnuts for a sweet-and-savory twist. To keep the spinach vivid green, heap it in a colander and drain the hot pasta right over it.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve in warmed bowls with extra olive oil and red pepper flakes at the table, alongside crusty bread or a simple salad.
