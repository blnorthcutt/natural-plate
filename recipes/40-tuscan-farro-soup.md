## Description

Simple yet amazing: this healthy soup, a kind of minestrone with farro, is ubiquitous in Lucca, a city in Tuscany. Chewy farro and creamy white beans simmer slowly with tomatoes and vegetables in a fragrant broth until the whole pot turns thick and comforting. It is the definition of a one-pot winter meal — hearty enough to be dinner on its own, and even better the next day. The farro is traditional, but spelt or barley work beautifully in its place.

## Instructions

1. **Sauté the aromatics.** Put the oil in a large, deep saucepan over medium heat; a minute later add the onion, celery, carrots, a large pinch of salt and some pepper. Cook, stirring, until the vegetables are glossy and the onion is softened, 5 to 10 minutes.
2. **Add the remaining ingredients.** Add the garlic and stir; add the farro, beans, tomatoes and stock, and stir to combine.
3. **Simmer low and slow.** Bring to a boil, then adjust the heat so the mixture simmers steadily. Cook until the farro and beans are tender, at least an hour, adding stock or water as necessary if the mixture becomes too thick.
4. **Finish with fresh herbs.** Stir in the parsley and basil (if using), then cook another 5 minutes. Taste and adjust the seasoning, then serve with lots of grated Parmesan.

## Notes

- **Bean tip (reader-tested):** The tomatoes' acidity can keep dried beans rock-hard — several readers soaked beans 10 hours and still found them crunchy after an hour of simmering. The fix: use canned white beans (rinsed; one reader uses their canning liquid too for a creamier soup) and simmer just 30 minutes after adding the farro, or cook dried beans separately before adding to the pot.
- **Storage:** Keeps in the fridge for up to 5 days and freezes beautifully for up to 3 months — portion into containers, as it reheats into the perfect quick dinner.
- **Variations:** Add a handful of chopped kale in the last few minutes (a Tuscan staple, per the comments). Toss in a Parmesan rind, a bay leaf, or a splash of white wine while simmering for extra depth. Mild Italian sausage and/or summer corn are crowd-pleasing additions.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve with lots of freshly grated Parmesan and crusty bread for dunking.
