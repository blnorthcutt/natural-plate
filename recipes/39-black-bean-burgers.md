## Description

Known in some circles as McBitty's Bean Burgers, these meatless patties from Mark Bittman get deep, savory flavor from dried porcini mushrooms, smoked paprika (or chili powder) and a splash of soy sauce, with black beans and rolled oats giving them body. They crisp up beautifully in a skillet in about 10 minutes and hold their own with all the usual burger fixings — no grill required. Perfect for a quick weeknight dinner or a batch of make-ahead frozen patties, and easy to pull together with pantry staples and a can of beans.

## Instructions

1. **Soak the mushrooms.** Put the dried porcini in a small bowl, cover with hot water and let soak for 5 to 10 minutes until softened; drain (reserving the liquid if you like) and roughly chop.
2. **Process the mixture.** Put the chopped mushrooms, beans, garlic, oats, spices and soy sauce in a food processor with a sprinkle of salt and pepper. Pulse until the mixture is combined but not puréed — a few pulses is usually plenty; err on the side of chunky so the beans stay toothsome. (No food processor? Use a potato masher.) If the mixture looks too wet, add a little more oats; if too dry, add a splash of bean-cooking liquid, porcini soaking liquid or water.
3. **Add the cilantro and rest.** Stir in the chopped cilantro and let the mixture sit for 5 minutes so the oats can absorb moisture.
4. **Shape the patties.** Form the mixture into 4 large or 8 small patties and let them sit for 5 minutes so they hold together better.
5. **Cook.** Put the oil in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Cook the patties until crisp and golden on one side, about 5 minutes, then flip and cook until crisp on the other side, another 5 minutes or so. Serve with the usual fixings.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Cooked patties keep in the fridge for up to 4 days. Uncooked patties freeze beautifully — stack them between sheets of parchment and freeze for up to 3 months; cook straight from frozen, adding a few minutes per side.
- **Make-ahead:** The mixture can be shaped into patties a day ahead and refrigerated, covered, until you're ready to cook.
- **Variations:** Readers report that pulsing the rolled oats in the food processor first makes them soak up more liquid and disappear into the patties while keeping the beans coarser and more toothsome. Doubling the spices and adding a spoonful of Dijon mustard gives the burgers a bigger flavor punch. No cilantro? Use flat-leaf parsley and chives instead. If using fresh mushrooms, use roughly 70 g fresh where the recipe calls for 7 g dried, and sauté them with a little onion first. When cooking beans from dried, simmer them with a bay leaf, a crushed garlic clove, a dried chili or fresh herbs to build flavor from the start.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve on toasted buns with lettuce, tomato, red onion, avocado, and your favorite burger sauces, or crumble a patty over a salad or grain bowl. Save the porcini soaking liquid to enrich a pot of lentils. Pat the patties dry with a paper towel if they feel wet before frying — it helps them crisp up and stay intact.
