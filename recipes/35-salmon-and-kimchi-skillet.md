## Description

Sautéing kimchi brings out its mellower side: a delicious, cabbage-y sweetness. In this lightning-fast skillet dinner, jarred cabbage kimchi is cooked in a fragrant mix of butter and toasted sesame oil with just a touch of sugar, creating a four-ingredient sauce that coats salmon fillets as they roast and broil together. The kimchi softens and deepens into caramelized, browned spots under the broiler while the salmon stays tender and moist. Serve over steamed rice to catch every drop of the savory-sweet pan sauce. A 20-minute weeknight stunner that tastes far more complex than its ingredient list suggests.

## Instructions

1. **Heat the oven.** Heat the oven to 450°F. Melt the butter with the sesame oil in a 12-inch oven-safe skillet over high heat.
2. **Cook the kimchi.** When the butter's foam subsides, add the kimchi with its liquid to the skillet. Stir in the sugar. Cook, stirring occasionally, until most of the liquid has bubbled away and the kimchi has softened, 3 to 5 minutes. If the liquid simmers away too quickly before the kimchi has softened, decrease the heat to medium and stir often.
3. **Add the salmon.** Season the salmon lightly with salt — go easy, the kimchi is already salty. Nestle the fillets in the skillet, skin-side down if your fillets are skin-on, spooning kimchi on top of the salmon. Roast in the oven for 6 minutes.
4. **Broil and serve.** Turn the broiler to high. Place the skillet on a rack about 5 inches from the broiler heat source and broil, turning and checking the fish and kimchi once, until the kimchi is very deeply browned in spots, 3 to 4 minutes. Serve topped with chopped scallions and sesame seeds, if you like.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best enjoyed fresh. Leftover salmon and kimchi keeps refrigerated in an airtight container for up to 2 days; reheat gently to avoid drying out the fish.
- **Salt sparingly:** Several readers note that kimchi is already quite salty, so season the salmon lightly — or skip the salt altogether and let the kimchi do the work.
- **Variations:** Add a splash of lemon juice or a sprinkle of gochugaru (Korean chile flakes) before broiling for extra brightness and heat. For more vegetables, toss in a handful of chopped purple cabbage along with the kimchi. Green onion kimchi works beautifully in place of cabbage kimchi.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve over steamed rice or with noodles and sautéed spinach to soak up the savory pan sauce.
