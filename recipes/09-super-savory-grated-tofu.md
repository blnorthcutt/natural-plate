## Description

Grating super-firm tofu is one of the best party tricks in the kitchen. It makes tofu not only quicker to prepare (no pressing required!) but also deceptively meaty. Here, grated tofu gets pan-fried until golden and crisp-edged, then tossed in a glossy, spicy, deeply savory Korean-inspired sauce. Served over rice with scallions and cilantro, it's hard to believe it isn't chicken — in the best possible way.

## Instructions

1. **Cook the rice.** Start by cooking the rice using your preferred method, or use leftover cooked rice.
2. **Grate the tofu.** Wrap the tofu in a thin dish towel and gently squeeze with your palms to remove some water — don't squish it. Using the large holes of a box grater, grate the tofu. If small pieces break off, slice them very thinly.
3. **Start the aromatics.** In a large nonstick skillet, heat the oil over medium-high heat. After a minute or two, add the scallions, chiles (if using), and garlic. Cook, stirring frequently, until the garlic is slightly golden and the scallions are softened, about 2 minutes.
4. **Brown the tofu.** Add the grated tofu to the pan and toss to coat it in the oil. Cook undisturbed for 2 minutes, then stir. Cook, stirring every 2 minutes, until the tofu is golden brown in some spots, 10 to 14 minutes total.
5. **Make the sauce.** Meanwhile, in a small bowl, whisk together the tamari, vinegar, sugar, gochugaru, and sesame oil until well combined.
6. **Finish.** Pour the sauce into the pan — it will bubble rapidly — and stir with a silicone spatula to evenly coat the tofu. Cook for 1 minute. Remove from the heat and sprinkle with the sesame seeds.
7. **Serve.** Serve over cooked rice and top with the reserved scallion tops and cilantro.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for 4 to 5 days.
- **Variations:** Super-firm or "high-protein" tofu works best, but extra-firm tofu pressed for 10 minutes also works. No gochugaru? Substitute 1 to 1½ teaspoons Sichuan chile flakes or sriracha. For a gluten-free version, use tamari and substitute the Chinese black vinegar with 2 parts rice vinegar to 1 part aged balsamic.
- **Serving suggestion:** Also delicious with noodles stir-fried with baby bok choy, or crumbled over cucumber.
