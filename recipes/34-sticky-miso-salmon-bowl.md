## Description

Miso salmon is an easy meal for any night of the week, but it gets taken to a whole other level here with the additions of grapefruit and honey. Combining the zest and juice from the grapefruit with honey, miso and a bit of ginger gives the fish a sticky-tangy finish when broiled. The sushi rice is mixed with a humble pat of butter and some sliced scallions, making it a comforting counterpart to this simple fish for an elegant weeknight dinner. Serve it with any combination of crunchy, spicy, and briny toppings — cucumber, avocado, chile crisp, kimchi, toasted nori — to build your perfect bowl.

## Instructions

1. **Rinse the rice.** Put the rice in a medium bowl and fill with cool tap water. Run your fingers through the rice, gently swooshing the grains around to loosen the starch. Dump out as much water as you can and repeat until the water runs slightly more clear, another two to three rinses.
2. **Cook the rice.** Drain the rice and transfer to a small or medium saucepan that has a tight-fitting lid. Pour in 2¼ cups cool water and bring to a boil over medium-high. Give the rice a stir to help keep it from sticking to the bottom of the pot, then cover and decrease heat to low. Cook without lifting the lid for 18 minutes. (Set your timer!)
3. **Make the marinade.** While the rice is cooking, place a rack about 5 inches from the broiler heat source and set the broiler to high. Whisk the miso, honey, oil, ginger and grapefruit zest and juice in a large bowl. Season the salmon lightly with salt and add to the bowl. Gently toss to coat. Marinate at room temperature until the timer for the rice goes off. (You can marinate the salmon for hours or a day ahead, but the paste is so concentrated that it works beautifully right away.)
4. **Let the rice steam.** Remove the pot of rice from the heat and let steam, covered, for 10 minutes, while you cook the salmon.
5. **Broil the salmon.** Using tongs, arrange the salmon on a foil-lined rimmed sheet tray. Make sure to leave the marinade on and spread any excess on top of the fillets — this makes for better browning. Broil until glossy and charred in most spots, about 5 minutes for medium-rare or 7 minutes for medium. Your timing will also depend on how much char you'd like on top.
6. **Fluff the rice and serve.** Uncover the rice and add the scallions and butter. Season with salt and several grinds of pepper. Fluff the rice with a rubber spatula until each grain is coated. Serve the salmon over the rice and add any of the toppings you desire.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best enjoyed fresh. Leftover salmon and rice keep refrigerated in an airtight container for up to 2 days; reheat gently to avoid drying out the fish.
- **Make-ahead:** The marinade can be prepared and frozen with portioned salmon fillets in Ziploc bags — set one out to thaw during the day and it's ready to cook for dinner. Several readers swear by this for easy weeknight meals.
- **Variations:** Lime or lemon work beautifully in place of grapefruit. No citrus on hand? Substitute rice vinegar and bake the salmon for 15 minutes at 450°F before finishing under the broiler. For extra flavor in the rice, several readers recommend adding 3 tablespoons of white miso to the cooking water. The toppings are endlessly adaptable — try furikake, toasted sesame seeds, or pickled vegetables in place of the suggested garnishes.
- **Serving suggestion:** Build your bowl with contrasting textures — cool cucumber, creamy avocado, a spoonful of chile crisp for heat, and crumbled toasted nori or furikake for a savory finish.
