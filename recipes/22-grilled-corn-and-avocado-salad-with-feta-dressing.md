## Description

This lively salad from NYT Cooking is summer on a plate: sweet grilled corn, charred scallions and jalapeño, and creamy avocado slices tossed with a tangy buttermilk-feta dressing. The sweetness of peak-summer corn and the richness of avocado balance out the tartness of the dressing, which gets its kick from the finely chopped charred jalapeño stirred right in. It comes together in 30 minutes — most of it grilling — and works as a light dinner or a showstopping side at a cookout.

## Instructions

1. **Heat the grill.** Heat a grill or grill pan over medium-high.
2. **Prep and oil the vegetables.** Brush the corn, scallions and jalapeño with the olive oil and season with salt and pepper.
3. **Grill the vegetables.** Arrange on the grill and cook, turning occasionally, until the corn kernels are browned in spots, 6 to 8 minutes, and the scallions and jalapeño are charred all over and tender, 9 to 10 minutes. Transfer to a cutting board and let cool slightly.
4. **Make the feta dressing.** In a medium bowl, using a whisk, mash the feta into a coarse paste. Whisk in the buttermilk, lemon zest and juice, and garlic, then stir in the chives and parsley. Finely chop the charred jalapeño (seeded first if you prefer less heat — the recipe keeps the seeds) and stir it into the dressing. Season with salt and pepper.
5. **Assemble the salad.** In a large bowl, toss the lettuce with half the feta dressing and arrange it on a platter. Cut the corn kernels off the cobs and slice the grilled scallions into bite-size pieces. Arrange the avocado slices, corn and scallions on top of the lettuce. Serve with the remaining dressing on the side.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best eaten the day it's made — the avocado will brown and the lettuce will wilt. Leftovers keep, undressed, in the fridge for up to a day.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve as a light dinner with crusty bread, or as a side for grilled chicken, steak, or burgers. The dressing also makes a fantastic dip or sauce for grilled vegetables.
- **Variations:** Many NYT commenters recommend swapping the romaine for kale (dino or lacinato) — it holds up better and doesn't get soggy — and subbing cilantro for the parsley for more flavor. Greek yogurt mixed with a little lemon juice works well in place of buttermilk. 
- **Equipment note:** If you don't have a grill, a blazing-hot cast-iron pan or grill pan works beautifully (see comments). Cut kernels off the cob and char them, tossing with a wooden spoon, until browned but still a bit crunchy.
