## Description

Cowboy caviar — also known as Texas caviar — is the Southern potluck classic credited to Helen Corbitt, who combined black-eyed peas with a simple vinaigrette during her years directing food service at Dallas's Neiman Marcus Zodiac Room. This NYT Cooking version from the archives tosses black beans, black-eyed peas, corn, tomato, bell pepper, and jalapeño in a bright red wine vinegar dressing, then lets everything chill for a couple of hours so the flavors meld. It's a no-cook, make-ahead crowd-pleaser that's just as at home at a summer cookout as it is at a fall tailgate, scooped up with a pile of tortilla chips.

## Instructions

1. **Make the dressing.** In a medium bowl, whisk the olive oil, red wine vinegar, minced garlic, sugar, ½ teaspoon salt, and ½ teaspoon pepper until well combined.
2. **Add the vegetables and beans.** Add the tomatoes, red onion, black beans, black-eyed peas, corn, bell pepper, jalapeño, and cilantro. Toss to combine, then season with salt and pepper to taste. Cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 2 hours before serving so the flavors can meld.
3. **Serve.** Toss well and re-season to taste. Sprinkle with the chopped scallion and a little extra cilantro if you like, and serve with tortilla chips for scooping.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Keeps in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 4 to 5 days — the flavors only get better after a day or two.
- **Make-ahead:** The perfect party recipe: the 2-hour chill is the minimum, but it can be made up to a day ahead and refrigerated overnight.
- **Variations:** Many commenters swap lime juice for the red wine vinegar — it pairs better with the black beans and complements diced avocado added just before serving. Roasting the corn kernels on a sheet pan with a little oil brings a toastier flavor. A can of drained corn simplifies things nicely. In Virginia mountain country, this is made as "Mountain Caviar" with halved cherry tomatoes and pinto beans instead of black beans.
- **Serving suggestion:** Serve with sturdy tortilla chips for scooping, or alongside burgers, grilled chicken, or pulled pork at a cookout. Texans say black-eyed peas bring good luck on New Year's — serve it then, too.
