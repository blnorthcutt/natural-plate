## Description

Fluffy, airy, golden-fried vegan doughnuts with a glossy glaze — the centerpiece of a Kwanzaa feast from Brooklyn baker Maati Kheprimeni Angaza. Made with almond milk, vegan butter and applesauce, the dough is sweet but not too sweet, and the sugar glaze can be left snowy white, turned chocolate with cocoa, or tinted festively with natural green, red or black food coloring. They fry up in minutes and are best the same day with a mug of coffee or tea — a joyful holiday treat that bridges new traditions and old.

## Instructions

1. **Bloom the yeast.** Stir the yeast and ¼ teaspoon of the granulated sugar into the lukewarm water in a small bowl. Let stand until foamy, about 5 minutes.
2. **Mix the dough.** Meanwhile, combine the almond milk, melted vegan butter, vanilla, salt and remaining ¼ cup (50 grams) granulated sugar in a large mixer bowl and stir well. Add the applesauce and the foamy yeast mixture, then beat with a hand mixer or a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment on medium speed until well blended.
3. **Add the flour.** With the mixer running on low speed, add the flour ¼ cup (30 grams) at a time, waiting until no floury streaks remain before adding the next. Continue beating until all traces of flour disappear.
4. **Knead and first rise.** With floured hands, turn the dough onto a generously floured surface and knead lightly, flouring the dough and surface as needed, until you have a ball that's tacky and elastic but not sticky. Place it in a large oiled bowl, cover with plastic wrap and let rise until doubled in size, about 1½ hours.
5. **Shape the doughnuts.** Transfer the risen dough to a floured work surface and sprinkle with flour. With a floured rolling pin, roll into a 13-by-10-inch rectangle about a scant inch thick. Using a floured 3-inch doughnut or round cutter, cut rounds as close together as possible; with a floured 1-inch cutter, stamp the centers. Gather the scraps, reroll and cut again. Place each doughnut with its hole on a small square of parchment or wax paper, spacing them an inch apart.
6. **Second rise.** Cover the doughnuts lightly with plastic wrap and let rise until puffed, about 45 minutes.
7. **Heat the oil.** Fill a large heavy pot with oil to a depth of 2 inches and heat over medium-high to 360°F. Line two wire racks with paper towels.
8. **Fry.** Carefully flip a doughnut and its hole off the parchment into the hot oil, repeating until the pot is almost full but not crowded. Fry, turning once and adjusting the heat to keep the oil at 360°F, until golden brown — 2 to 4 minutes for doughnuts and 1 to 2 minutes for holes. Drain on the paper towels, letting the oil come back to temperature between batches, then cool both to room temperature.
9. **Make the glaze.** Whisk the confectioners' sugar, vanilla and ¼ cup (60 ml) almond milk in a medium bowl until smooth. For a chocolate glaze, stir in the cocoa powder; for a colored glaze, tint with green, red or black food coloring. Thin with more almond milk, a tablespoon at a time, if needed.
10. **Glaze and serve.** Discard the paper towels and set the racks over rimmed baking sheets. Dip the tops of the doughnuts and holes in the glaze and return them to the rack, sprinkling walnuts over any chocolate-glazed ones. Let stand until the glaze sets, then serve the same day.

## Notes

- **Storage:** These are best served the day they're made; readers report they're still tasty the next morning, but the glaze and crispness fade quickly after that.
- **Make-ahead:** One reader cold-proofs the shaped doughnuts in the fridge overnight before the second rise — pull them out in the morning, let them come to room temperature and finish rising (about 2 hours), then fry; they still fried beautifully.
- **Variations:** The recipe author confirms the doughnuts work in an air fryer. Swap the almond milk for any nondairy milk, or use dairy milk and butter if you're not vegan. Skip the glaze entirely and toss warm doughnuts in cinnamon sugar, or serve them with jam for dipping. A note on gluten-free baking: readers report that risen yeast doughs don't come out as well with gluten-free flour blends — a cake doughnut is a better bet there.
- **Serving suggestion:** Festive for Kwanzaa, Hanukkah or holiday brunch — glaze them in green, red and black for a Kwanzaa spread. Watch the oil temperature: if it creeps too hot, the doughnuts can brown in 20 seconds flat, so keep a thermometer in the pot.
