## Description

Inspired by the many cold silken tofu dishes of East Asia — Japanese hiyayakko and Chinese liangban tofu among them — this no-cook dish drapes silky, chilled tofu in a punchy soy dressing brightened with sesame oil, chile oil, vinegar, and toasted sesame seeds, then finishes it with scallions and cilantro. It's the recipe to have up your sleeve for warm evenings when the desire to cook is nonexistent: five minutes, one bowl, zero heat. Serve it cold on its own, or over hot rice or noodles for a pleasing contrast of temperatures.

## Instructions

1. **Make the dressing.** In a small bowl, combine the soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil, chile oil, sesame seeds, sugar, and half the sliced scallion. Whisk until the sugar has dissolved.
2. **Drain and dry the tofu.** Carefully pour off the liquid from the tofu packages and gently tip each block onto a clean kitchen towel. (Try to keep the blocks intact, but don't worry if they crack or fall apart — it still tastes great.) Pat with a second clean towel, pressing gently to remove as much liquid as possible so the dressing clings.
3. **Plate and dress.** Transfer the blocks to one large plate or two smaller plates — leave whole or cut into 1-inch pieces — and spoon the dressing over the top until the tofu is completely covered.
4. **Garnish and serve.** Top with the remaining sliced scallion and the cilantro leaves, and eat on its own or with rice or noodles on the side.

## Notes

- **Storage:** Best eaten right away — the dressing makes the tofu progressively saltier as it sits. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 1 day; spoon any pooled dressing from the bottom of the plate back over before serving.
- **Make-ahead:** Whisk the dressing up to a day ahead and refrigerate it in an airtight container, shaking it well before use. Only drain the tofu at serving time.
- **Variations:** Many commenters swear by adding a little freshly grated ginger. For a milder, more Cantonese-style dressing, dilute the soy sauce with an equal amount of water. Adjust the chile oil to taste — it works with more, less, or none at all. Swap the cilantro for Thai basil, mint, or shiso leaves, add crunch with fried shallots or roasted peanuts, or tuck in a spoonful of kimchi or pickled radish.
- **Serving suggestion:** One block of silken tofu feeds about two people, so scale up for a crowd. Commenters love topping the tofu with whatever crisp vegetables are on hand — cucumber, radishes, snap peas, avocado, or baby spinach.
